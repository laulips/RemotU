Accessibility reference · v1

Designing for people relearning to move, speak and remember

The rules behind this prototype, for anyone building on it. Each rule says what it is, why it exists, and where to find it in the app. The last section lists what is not done yet.

Three constraints shape everything

01 · APHASIA
Reading, naming and word-finding may be impaired, while understanding of images and numbers often survives.
  • Short labels (1–3 words) next to every icon
  • Answers are taps, never typed text
  • Quick replies replace free text in messages
  • No exercise requires reading or naming
02 · FATIGUE
Post-stroke and post-TBI fatigue is common and unpredictable. A session that runs too long gets abandoned.
  • Sessions are ≤15 min, shown up front
  • Energy check-in comes before exercise
  • Exit is always available, top-left
  • Progress is saved after every set
03 · COGNITIVE LOAD
Attention and working memory are reduced. Every extra choice on screen costs effort.
  • One question or task per screen
  • One primary action, full width, at the bottom
  • Segmented progress bar shows how far along you are
  • No time limits; errors are worded gently

Interaction patterns

Help in the exercise
“Come si fa?” is always in the player header. It opens the step-by-step guide, pauses the timer, and returns to the same set.
Patients see less
The exercise guide hides spinal segments and clinical tags from patients. They see only the video, purpose and steps.
Three player styles
Focus (one giant button), Coach (audio cue and timer), Ritmo (external pacing). Pick per patient. Ritmo suits gait and cadence work.
Gentle feedback
“Quasi. Ecco quelle giuste” instead of “Sbagliato”. The correct answer is always shown. Adherence mechanics never punish a missed day.
Motion
Pulse, sway and breathing animations switch off when the system’s “reduce motion” setting is on.
